This project develops a **spatio-temporal building dataset covering 8,155 officially recognized traditional villages across China**, with observations for 2015, 2020, and 2025. It aims to provide consistent, building-level spatial data for investigating the morphological organization and transformation of historic rural settlements.

The workflow integrates **object detection, image segmentation, historical change detection, and multi-source geospatial data fusion**. Building footprints are extracted from high-resolution remote-sensing imagery using a detection-assisted segmentation pipeline. Historical satellite imagery is then used to identify which buildings in the 2025 reference dataset were already present in earlier years, enabling the reconstruction of their observed persistence across the three time points.

Additional geospatial information supports spatial alignment, quality assessment, and the characterization of building and settlement morphology. The resulting dataset provides a structured basis for comparative analysis of traditional village morphology and spatial transformation at a national scale.

As a methodological demonstration, this project illustrates how computer vision, multi-temporal remote sensing, and geospatial data integration can support the systematic documentation and computational study of built heritage.
