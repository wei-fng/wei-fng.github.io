### Abstract

Living historic settlements retain inherited spatial order while continuing to be shaped by changes in geographic, cultural, and socio-economic contexts. In this regard, similarities among such settlements extend beyond visible form and depend on how spatial configuration relates to broader contextual conditions. Such a relational understanding is important for heritage interpretation and comparative approaches to settlement conservation. Yet existing comparisons often privilege visible morphology or symbolic features, leaving less visible relational similarities underexamined. This study therefore asks whether recurring relations among spatial configurations and contextual conditions can be interpreted as contextually embedded convergent settlement logics in living historic settlements.
This study examines 8,155 Chinese traditional villages as living historic settlements, situating them within China's evolving policy context of heritage conservation and rural revitalization. It draws on multi-temporal satellite imagery, OpenStreetMap data, and complementary contextual data at three time points between 2015 and 2025 to construct an integrated spatio-temporal heterogeneous graph representation. The analysis is organized around two complementary levels. The first focuses on the internal structure of each village, where graph relations are used to represent how built elements are connected with mobility structures. The second focuses on relationships between villages, where a similarity network is constructed to compare their contextual conditions, including natural geography, modern transport accessibility, agricultural-cultural setting, and industrial background. Based on these two levels of representation, embedding-based clustering is used to identify candidate groups of villages with similar relational patterns, while interpretable graph-based analysis is used to explain these groups in relation to spatial structure and contextual variables.
The comparisons indicate that visible similarity and graph-derived relational similarity do not always coincide. Villages with comparable outward morphology or plan configurations may differ in the relations among roads, buildings, and boundaries, whereas visually different villages may share patterns of accessibility, aggregation, and boundary formation. At the inter-village level, the combined analysis of intra-village graph representations and the contextual similarity network suggests that settlement similarity can emerge from the joint effects of internal spatial organization and contextual affinities in geography, agrarian culture, and industrial background. Longitudinal comparisons add a temporal dimension: visible spatial configuration may remain stable, while graph-derived spatial relations indicate reorganization associated with changes in modern transport connectivity. Together, these results frame similarity as a relational condition produced through spatial order, environmental setting, mobility, and socio-economic transformation.
Overall, the findings suggest that similarities among living historic settlements are expressed not only through visible morphology or regional typology, but also through the relational organization of spatial elements and their connections to contextual conditions. They also highlight the value of relational graph analysis in identifying changes that may be overlooked by morphological comparisons. Grounded in the context of Chinese traditional villages, the study contributes empirical cases to broader discussions on settlement protection and development, while suggesting a relational approach to documentation and regional comparison that may inform comparative studies in global contexts.
