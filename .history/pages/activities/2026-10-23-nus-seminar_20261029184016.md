### Knowledge Modelling for Built Heritage & Conservation

Organized by [Reasonable Design Lab (RDL)](https://www.linkedin.com/company/reasonable-design-lab/), National University of Singapore.

### Invited Research Presentation

**From Morphology to Relations: A Spatio-Temporal Graph Study of 8,155 Chinese Traditional Villages**

**Speaker:** Wei Feng · Southeast University  
**Date:** 23 October 2026  

#### Abstract

This study asks a fundamental question: in traditional villages as living built heritage, is similarity between settlements defined only by visible morphology, or can it also emerge from relations embedded in spatial organization and broader environmental and socio-economic contexts?

Focusing on 8,155 Chinese traditional villages, we integrate multi-temporal satellite imagery, road networks, and environmental and socio-economic data from 2015 to 2025. We construct heterogeneous graphs to represent buildings, roads, and their spatial relations within individual villages, together with inter-village networks capturing geographic, cultural, and industrial affinities.

Through graph representation learning and clustering, we examine where morphological similarity converges with—or diverges from—relational similarity, and how village spatial representations change across contextual relations and over time. The study suggests that similarities among traditional settlements are expressed not only through visible form, but also through relational structures jointly shaped by spatial organization, environmental conditions, and socio-economic processes.
